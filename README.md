Maruti Suzuki India: 5-Year Marketing & Finance Analysis (FY2022–FY2026) and Outlook (FY2027E–FY2031E)
A self-project for my PGDM (Marketing + Finance) that studies how Maruti Suzuki India Limited performed over the last five completed financial years, how its advertising spend relates to financial results, and what the next five years could look like under three scenarios.
Disclaimer: This is an academic project. It is not financial or investment advice, and I am not a registered financial advisor. Forecasts are my own scenario estimates, not company guidance.
Objectives
Track the trend of Maruti's advertising spend over FY2022–FY2026.
Test whether ad spend is related to revenue, EBITDA, net profit and vehicle volume.
Understand how the SUV shift, exports and sales channels affected profitability.
Suggest ways to improve marketing ROI.
Build base, bull and bear forecasts for FY2027E–FY2031E.

Growth was real: volume rose about 47% and net sales more than doubled.
Profit stalled in FY26: profit rose only about 1% while sales grew about 20%, because of commodity costs, forex, promotions, new-plant start-up costs and one-time items.
Advertising stayed near 1.1% of revenue for four years. In FY26 it fell 6% while revenue rose about 20%, so FY26 growth came mainly from the GST 2.0 tax cut, not from extra advertising.
Product mix changed: SUVs/UVs rose from about 22% of Maruti's domestic car sales (FY23) to about 41% (Apr–Dec FY26).

Limitations
Only five annual data points, so statistical tests have low power.
Advertising figures come from a data vendor's classification and may differ from the Annual Report note.
Market share figures come from mixed sources (SIAM wholesale, FADA retail, company statements).
Standalone vs consolidated figures and the SMG merger restatement cause small differences.
External shocks (chip shortage, COVID, GST 2.0, West Asia war) affect results independently of marketing.
Forecasts will be wrong in detail and should be updated when new results arrive.
Terms used
EBITDA: operating profit before depreciation, interest, other income and tax.
bps / pp: 100 basis points = 1 percentage point.
CAGR: compound annual growth rate.
Pearson r: a number from -1 to +1 showing how closely two series move together.
